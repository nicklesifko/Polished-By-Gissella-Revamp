Keep Them Fresh

Nail Aftercare.

Simple tips to protect your investment and keep your set looking its best between appointments.

Right After Your Appointment

What should I do immediately after my appointment?

+

Your nails are fully cured when you leave, so you can go about your day normally. However, for the first few hours:

  • Avoid prolonged soaking in water (long baths, dishes, swimming)
  • Be gentle with your nails — the product needs time to fully bond to your natural nail
  • Avoid picking at or peeling the edges
Tip: Apply cuticle oil as soon as you get home — it helps the product settle and keeps the surrounding skin hydrated.

Why do I need to avoid water right after?

+

Your natural nail can absorb water and expand slightly. In the first 24–48 hours, excess moisture under or around the product can weaken the adhesion and cause early lifting. The drier you keep them right after your appointment, the longer your set will last.

Daily Care

How often should I apply cuticle oil?

+

At least once a day — ideally morning and night. Cuticle oil keeps the skin around your nails hydrated, prevents peeling and cracking, and helps maintain the flexibility of the product so it's less prone to chipping.

Tip: Keep a small cuticle oil pen in your bag so you can apply it throughout the day.

Should I wear gloves for household tasks?

+

Yes — especially for:

  • Washing dishes
  • Cleaning with chemicals or harsh products
  • Gardening or tasks involving dirt and grit

Prolonged water exposure and cleaning chemicals are the two biggest causes of early lifting and chipping. A cheap pair of rubber gloves will make your set last noticeably longer.

Can I use hand lotion and sanitiser?

+

Hand lotion is fine and actually encouraged — just massage it into your hands and avoid rubbing it heavily around the nail edges. Hand sanitiser is also fine for occasional use, but the alcohol content can dry out the cuticles over time. Follow up with cuticle oil if you use it frequently.

What to Avoid

What can shorten the life of my set?

+
  • Using your nails as tools (opening cans, peeling stickers, etc.)
  • Picking or peeling at the product — this damages your natural nail
  • Prolonged soaking in water (hot tubs, long baths)
  • Harsh cleaning chemicals without gloves
  • Acetone-based products near the nail edges
  • Biting or chewing on your nails
Tip: Use the pad of your finger, not the tip of your nail, whenever possible.

Can I use regular nail polish on top?

+

Yes, but use a non-acetone remover when taking it off. Regular acetone polish remover will break down gel and hard gel products. A gentle non-acetone remover is safe to use on the polish without affecting your set underneath.

Dealing with Breakage

What do I do if a nail breaks or lifts?

+

Don't panic — and don't try to fix it yourself by peeling or pulling. Here's what to do:

  • If it's a minor lift at the edge, keep it dry and DM me on Instagram — we can assess if it needs a quick fix
  • If a nail breaks close to the nail bed, keep it covered and reach out as soon as possible
  • Never peel or force the product off — this strips layers of your natural nail
Tip: DM @PolishedByGissella on Instagram with a photo and I'll let you know if it needs an in-person fix or can wait until your next appointment.

Is some lifting normal?

+

Minor lifting at the very edge after 3–4 weeks is normal as your natural nail grows out. However, lifting within the first week typically points to one of the following:

  • Exposure to a lot of water in the first 24–48 hours
  • Oily natural nails (let me know before your appointment so I can prep accordingly)
  • Product contact with skin or cuticles during application

If you experience consistent early lifting, mention it at your next appointment so the prep process can be adjusted.

When to Come Back

How do I know when it's time for a refill?

+

Generally, refills are recommended every 3 weeks. Signs it's time to book:

  • Visible gap between the product and your cuticle
  • Noticeable lifting at the edges
  • Nails feel thinner or flexible near the base
  • It's been more than 5 weeks since your last appointment
Tip: Waiting too long between refills can make the removal process harder on your natural nails.

How many refills can I get before needing a full soak-off?

+

A maximum of 2 refills are allowed per set for Hard Gel and Soft Builder Gel. After that, a full soak-off and fresh set is required to maintain the health, strength, and appearance of your nails. Stacking too many refills can lead to over-filing and thin, weakened natural nails.

How do I care for my natural nails between sets?

+

If you're taking a break between sets:

  • Keep nails trimmed and filed to avoid snagging
  • Continue using cuticle oil daily
  • Use a nail strengthener or clear base coat to protect the nail surface
  • Stay hydrated — nail health starts from the inside
  • Avoid harsh chemicals and prolonged water exposure just as you would with a set
Tip: Give your natural nails at least 1–2 weeks to breathe and recover before booking your next set, especially if they feel thin or flexible.
